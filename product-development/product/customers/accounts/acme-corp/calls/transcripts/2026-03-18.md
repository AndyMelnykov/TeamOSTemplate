## Version Rollback Incident (2:00 - 14:30)

**Derek Liu:** So last Thursday — March 13th — one of our team members was working on our vendor contract renewal workflow. The one we published through the One-Click Publish beta. She'd been iterating on the routing logic and made a change that broke some field mapping. Normal stuff. She went to version history to roll back to the previous version.

**Hannah:** Okay.

**Derek Liu:** The rollback appeared to succeed. example_product showed the previous version restored, the preview looked right, the workflow re-published automatically. Everything seemed fine. But 40 minutes later, our ops lead gets a Slack ping from Legal. A vendor contract had reached e-signature with no legal review and no finance sign-off. Three approval steps — the legal review step, the finance sign-off step, and the manager approval step — were just gone.

**Jordan Kim:** Gone as in the routing nodes were missing from the workflow config?

**Derek Liu:** Exactly. The rollback restored the intake form and most of the extraction rules, but it silently dropped three approval routing nodes. No error, no warning, no diff showing what changed. The team member had no way of knowing the rollback was incomplete. The contract sat 40 minutes away from being fully executed before anyone noticed, and it took another 20 minutes to figure out what happened and manually pull the routing config back from a backup.

**Jordan Kim:** That's a serious issue. Do you still have the workflow ID and the version timestamps? I want to reproduce this in our environment.

**Derek Liu:** Priya has all the details. She did the post-mortem.

**Priya Nair:** I'll send everything over today — workflow ID, the two version hashes, and our internal incident report. The root cause from our side is clear: the rollback didn't preserve the full routing tree. It restored the steps that existed in both versions but dropped steps that were added between the two versions and were still needed.

**Jordan Kim:** So it treated the rollback as a snapshot restore rather than a diff-based revert. That's a bug in how we handle step additions across versions. I can see how that would happen with our current implementation.

**Hannah:** Jordan, what's your confidence level that we can fix this?

**Jordan Kim:** High. This sounds like a specific edge case in how we serialize the routing manifest during rollback. I'll need to dig into it, but architecturally it's a solvable problem. The harder question is whether there are other edge cases like this we haven't found yet.

**Derek Liu:** And that's what worries me. We had a production incident last Thursday where an engineer tried to rollback to a previous version and it silently dropped three required approval steps. We were down forty minutes before anyone noticed. Version history can't just be a nice-to-have — it needs to be rock solid. If I can't trust rollback, I can't trust example_product for production workflows. We've published five workflows now. Every one of them could have this same issue.

**Hannah:** Derek, I hear you, and I want to validate that concern. You're right that rollback needs to be bulletproof if we're going to be a production platform. I'm going to escalate this internally as a P0 bug.

## Version Diff Discussion (14:30 - 21:00)

**Hannah:** Priya, you mentioned in the pre-call notes that the version history UI made this harder to catch. Can you walk us through that?

**Priya Nair:** Yeah. So when you open version history in example_product today, you see a list of versions. Each one has a timestamp and a label if you added one. That's it. There's no way to see what changed between version A and version B. No diff view, no step list, nothing.

**Jordan Kim:** You'd want something like a GitHub PR diff?

**Priya Nair:** Exactly. The version history UI shows me a list of timestamps, but I can't see what actually changed between versions. I need a diff view — show me what steps or fields were added, removed, modified. Without that I'm rolling back blind. If our team member had been able to see a diff before confirming the rollback, she would have noticed that three approval steps were about to be removed. She would have caught it before it went to production.

**Hannah:** That makes total sense. A diff view would serve as both a diagnostic tool and a safety check.

**Priya Nair:** Right. And beyond rollback, it's useful for general development. When I'm refining a workflow — instruction, extract, review, instruction again — I want to see what each refinement actually changed. Sometimes the model modifies steps I didn't ask it to touch. A diff view catches that.

**Jordan Kim:** We've had a version diff feature on the backlog for a while. I think this incident elevates the priority. Hannah, we should probably spec this out alongside the rollback fix.

**Hannah:** Agreed. I'll draft a spec this week and share it with Priya for feedback.

**Priya Nair:** Happy to review. I have a lot of opinions about what a good diff UX looks like. I've used GitHub, GitLab, and Bitbucket extensively and they all have different trade-offs.
