## Contract Review Workflow (2:30 - 12:45)

**Rachel Torres:** Sure. So the biggest thing on my mind right now is collaboration. We've grown to 15 people, and we're spread across three offices — Austin, Munich, and Shenzhen. Our supplier contract and purchase order review meetings are the heartbeat of our procurement process. Every week, we get people from all three sites on a call to review the latest workflows — PO extraction rules, contract routing, approval logic, all built in example_product.

**Hannah:** How are those review sessions going today?

**Rachel Torres:** Honestly? They're chaos. Here's what happens. We get on a Zoom, someone shares their screen, they walk through the workflow, people give feedback verbally, then they try to make edits while everyone watches. Then the next person shares their screen. It's like watching paint dry. A session that should be 30 minutes takes 90 because everything is sequential.

**Sam Chen:** Are you using the commenting feature at all for async feedback?

**Rachel Torres:** We tried. It doesn't work for us. Our review cadence is too fast. By the time someone leaves a comment and the other person reads it, the context has shifted. Procurement timelines are tight — we can't afford a 12-hour feedback loop between Austin and Shenzhen. We need everyone in the same canvas, editing at the same time, seeing each other's cursors. Real-time multiplayer, basically.

**Hannah:** That's really clear feedback. We've been hearing similar signals from other accounts, and real-time collaboration is actively in development. Sam, do you want to speak to where we are?

**Sam Chen:** Yeah, we've been prototyping real-time collaboration internally. It's early, but the architecture is solid — we're using CRDTs for conflict resolution, and we've got basic cursor presence working in our dev environment. We're probably 4 to 6 weeks from something we could show externally.

**Rachel Torres:** That would be incredible. Seriously, that's the feature that would change everything for us. Can we be first in line to test it?

**Hannah:** Absolutely. I'll make sure you're in the beta cohort.

## Serialized Editing Pain (12:45 - 19:00)

**Hannah:** Ben, Rachel mentioned the sequential workflow. Can you talk about what that looks like day to day for you?

**Ben Harlow:** Yeah, so Rachel and I work on the same workflows a lot. She owns the approval routing and legal review logic — signer groups, escalation paths, clause flags — and I own the extraction rules — pricing tables, delivery terms, line-item mapping. In a perfect world, we'd both be editing the same workflow at the same time. She's tweaking the routing steps, I'm adjusting the extraction fields, and we can see each other's work as it happens.

**Sam Chen:** And today?

**Ben Harlow:** Today it's fully serialized. I work on my part, save it, hand it to Rachel, she works on her part. Then we get on a call to merge everything together. If I could edit the extraction rules while Rachel reviews the approval routing, we'd cut our review cycles in half. Right now we're serialized — I wait for her, she waits for me. It's the biggest time sink in my week.

**Hannah:** How much time would you estimate you lose to that serialization?

**Ben Harlow:** Conservatively? Four to five hours a week across the team. And that's just the direct time. The context-switching cost is probably another two to three hours. You finish your work, move on to something else, then get pulled back when Rachel's ready. It breaks flow.

**Rachel Torres:** And multiply that by the number of workflows. We have 11 active workflows right now. Even if only half of them have multi-person editing needs, it adds up fast.
