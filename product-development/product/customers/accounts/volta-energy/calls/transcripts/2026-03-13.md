## Inline Field Mapping Discussion (4:30 - 15:00)

**Carlos Reyes:** Sure. So the biggest technical challenge we've hit is with inline field mapping on complex forms. I built an equipment inspection work order with 6 nested checklist sections. When I try to inline-edit one field mapping, the layout of the other 5 breaks. I end up rebuilding the whole form and losing my tweaks.

**Jordan Kim:** Can you describe the form structure? Is this a single flat checklist, sectioned panels, or a combination?

**Carlos Reyes:** It's an outer form with 3 columns and 2 rows of sections. Each cell contains a checklist block, and two of those cells have their own internal sub-grid for side-by-side measurement fields. So it's a form-within-form. When I click into one of the inner checklist blocks to edit a field mapping, example_product re-renders the containing section and the layout for the outer form shifts.

**Jordan Kim:** That's consistent with a known limitation in our inline field-mapping engine. The diffing algorithm currently operates at the page level rather than being scoped to the section you're editing. So when it patches in your edit, it recalculates layout for the entire form, which can cause cascade effects in nested section contexts.

**Carlos Reyes:** Is there a timeline for fixing that?

**Jordan Kim:** We're actively working on scoped section editing — the idea is that when you edit a checklist block, only that block's subtree gets re-rendered. It's technically challenging because of how nested section inheritance works, but it's a priority for us. In the meantime, I have a workaround that might help.

**Carlos Reyes:** I'm all ears.

**Jordan Kim:** Try decomposing your work order into isolated checklist components. Instead of one monolithic form with 6 inline sections, create 6 separate checklist blocks and then compose them in a parent layout. When you inline-edit a child block, the parent layout stays stable because the component boundary acts as a re-render barrier.

**Carlos Reyes:** I've actually been doing something similar, but the composition step is fragile. When I update a child block, I have to manually re-attach it to the parent and sometimes the parent's layout breaks anyway.

**Jordan Kim:** That shouldn't happen with the latest version — we shipped a fix for component re-attachment stability two weeks ago. Are you on the latest?

**Carlos Reyes:** I think so, but let me double-check. If the re-attachment is stable now, that would make the decomposition approach much more viable.

**Jordan Kim:** Let me send you a best practices doc on section decomposition for complex forms. I'll include examples that mirror your form-within-form pattern so you can adapt them directly.

**Carlos Reyes:** That would be incredibly helpful. Thanks, Jordan.
